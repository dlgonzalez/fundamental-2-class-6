Unit Intro: Are you a planner?

Start with your own habits. There are no right or wrong answers.

Question 1

Are you a planner or a last-minute person?

Do you plan everything, or do you decide what to do at the last minute? Why?

Question 2

What does your perfect free Saturday look like?

Do you make a schedule, or do you wake up and see what happens?

Pair-share

Think for 30 seconds. Share your answer with a partner and ask one follow-up question. Invite a few students to share with the class.

Useful starters: “I usually…”, “I prefer… because…”, “I don't like planning because…”, “It depends.”

Time & Connectors: When? In what order?

Give your routine a clear timeline and connect ideas into a smooth paragraph.

Learning goal

By the end of this section, you can use AT, ON, IN with time expressions and connect routine activities using sequence words and conjunctions.

Part A — Prepositions of time ⏰

🟣 AT = exact time / point

AT 6:30 · AT noon · AT midnight · AT night

“I wake up at 6:00.” “I study at night.”

A specific point on the clock or fixed time expression.

🟢 ON = days / dates

ON Monday · ON weekdays · ON July 4th

“I have English class on Tuesdays.” “I visit my grandparents on the weekend.”

A day on the calendar. American English: on the weekend; British English: at the weekend.

🟠 IN = longer periods

IN the morning · IN July · IN 2026

“I do homework in the afternoon.” “My birthday is in May.”

A period of time, month, year, season, or part of the day.

Important exceptions and useful patterns

No preposition: every day, every morning, next Monday, last night, this weekend. “I exercise every morning.”

Part C — Sequence words & connectors 🔗

Some expressions show order; others add, contrast, explain, or connect ideas.

➡️ Order / sequence

First — the first action

Then / Next — the following action

After that — the next stage

Later — at a later time

Finally — the last action

➕ Add information

And joins related ideas. Also adds another detail.

“I have breakfast and check my messages.” “I also prepare my bag.”

↔️ Contrast

But shows a different or contrasting idea.

“I like waking up early, but I don't like running.”

💡 Reason

Because explains why.

“I take the bus because my school is far away.”

🕒 Time / situation

When introduces the time something happens.

“When I get home, I have a snack.”

“I listen to music when I do chores.”

🧠 Punctuation tip

Use a comma after an opening sequence/time phrase: “After that, I get dressed.” “When I get home, I do homework.”

Do not automatically put a comma before every and or but.

Grammar Lab: The Simple Present

Use it to talk about routines, habits, repeated actions, and facts.

Learning goal

By the end, you can describe routines, ask and answer questions about habits, write about yourself, and report what another person does.

When do we use the Simple Present?

🔁 Routines & habits

I wake up at 6:00 every day.

📅 Repeated actions

She visits her grandmother on Sundays.

🌍 Facts

Water boils at 100°C.

❤️ Likes & preferences

He loves playing basketball.

1. Affirmative sentences ✅

I / You / We / They + base verb

He / She / It + verb-s / verb-es / verb-ies

I / You / We / They

I work at home. They study English. We go to school by bus.

He / She / It

He works at home. She studies English. My brother goes to school by bus.

Third-person spelling rules: he / she / it

Most verbs: + s

work → works · read → reads · play → plays

-s, -sh, -ch, -x, -o: + es

watch → watches · wash → washes · go → goes · fix → fixes

Consonant + y: y → ies

study → studies · try → tries

Vowel + y: play → plays

Special: have → has

2. Negative sentences ❌

I / You / We / They + don't + base verb

I don't drink coffee. They don't walk to school.

He / She / It + doesn't + base verb

She doesn't drink coffee. He doesn't walk to school.

Important: After doesn't, the main verb returns to its base form. ✓ She doesn't study. ✗ She doesn't studies.

3. Questions and short answers ❓

Do + I / you / we / they + base verb?

Do you play soccer? Yes, I do. / No, I don't.

Does + he / she / it + base verb?

Does she play soccer? Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.

Wh- questions: What time do you wake up? Where does he study? How often do they exercise?

Final Project: My Routine, Your Routine

Write, interview, collect information, and report.

Final learning outcome

Create a clear paragraph about your own routine and a short group report about classmates' routines, using the Simple Present, time expressions, prepositions, and connectors.

Part A — My routine paragraph ✍️

  1. Brainstorm. List 6–8 activities. Include morning, afternoon, and evening.
  2. Add time details. Use two clock times, two days / frequency expressions, and AT, IN, and ON where appropriate.
  3. Connect your ideas. Use at least three sequence words and two connectors from and, also, but, because, when.
  4. Write one paragraph. Use the Simple Present, a logical sequence, and a closing idea.
  5. Check your work. Review verb forms, spelling, punctuation, and clarity.
